Split flap and ASCII, running

Both concepts run here for real, driven by one expression rig. Expressions are recipes of FACS Action Units. The rig draws a plain "face field" from the current Action Units, and each look samples that field onto its own grid: the split-flap board picks a flap shape per tile and flips to it, and the ASCII face picks a character per cell. The controls stand in for the robot's /face/* topics; nothing here connects to ROS.

The pipeline

Topics/face/expression picks a recipe, /face/gaze moves the pupils, /face/mouth opens the jaw at 20 Hz.
→
AU rigThe recipe's Action Units blend in over about 200 ms. Blink (AU45) and speech (AU25 + AU26) are layered on top.
→
Face fieldA plain vector face drawn from AU values: brow ends, upper and lower lids, pupils, mouth corners, jaw.
→
SamplerSplit flap: each tile picks the flap shape closest to its 8×8 patch, then flips there. ASCII: each cell picks a character by brightness and edge direction.

How expressions would work

One rig, many looks

  • Expressions are defined once, as Action Unit recipes, never per look. Split flap, ASCII and any later look are samplers of the same face field.
  • /face/expression stays the preset name. A later /face/au topic (unit, intensity) could drive the rig directly; the presets are just named recipes.
  • A grid look can only show what survives sampling. At 24 × 14 tiles each eye is about 5 tiles wide, enough for slanted lids, a lifted lower lid and a pupil, but not for subtle AU2 or AU7 alone. Recipes should push those units harder or pair them.
  • "Afraid" isn't in the current topic list. It is shown here because the recipe (AU1+2+4+5+20+25+26) works in both looks without any per-look art.

Split flap mechanics

  • Every tile has a target flap shape: the alphabet shape with the smallest squared error against the tile's 8 × 8 patch of the face field. Its colour (cream eye or amber feature) comes from whichever covers more of the patch.
  • A tile changes only when the new shape is clearly better (hysteresis), so the board doesn't chatter while the face drifts.
  • A changing tile flips through up to two random shapes before landing, with per-tile jitter, which gives the cascading departures-board clatter. Mouth tiles flip straight to the target in 50 ms so speech keeps up with 20 Hz.
  • Gaze is quantised by the tiles: pupils hop a tile at a time with a flip, which reads as mechanical eye movement.
  • Optional sound: a short synthesized clack per landed flip, capped per frame.

ASCII mechanics

  • The face field, plus a shaded head and ear pieces, is sampled at 2 × 2 points per cell. Brightness picks from  .:-=+*#%@; strong edges pick _ / | \ by the edge's direction.
  • Eyes render amber and the rest phosphor green. Pupils are holes, so they read through the character density.
  • When the mouth opens, the cells inside it spell the line being spoken, scrolling as it talks. A future /face/say text topic could feed it real words.
  • Topic names drift down the empty columns behind the head. A small share of cells flicker to keep it alive at rest.
  • The same sampler is a cheap filter for any other look: it could run as a post-process over the Murmuration particles, for example.

For the agent defining expressions

ExpressionRecipe used here (intensity 0–1)
  • The rig reads 14 units: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 15, 18, 20, 24, 25, 26, 43, plus AU45 blink and continuous gaze in place of AU61–64. These are the parts a look must move independently.
  • "Sleepy" isn't a FACS emotion; it is AU43 held at about 0.7 with a slight AU1.
  • Open questions: left/right asymmetry (one brow, a wink), intensity scaling per expression, and whether blink suppression should key off AU43 rather than the name "sleepy".
